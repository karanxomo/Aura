I am writing in support of Project AURA, being developed by Armaan Jain, aged 11 who is representing India at the global Fibonacci competition.

My name is Dr Rakesh Gupta and I am a Senior consultant in Internal Medicine at the Apollo Indraprastha Hospital, New Delhi with over three decades of clinical experience. I reviewed a demonstration of the AURA prototype on 18th November 2026.

In my practice, I regularly see how cumulative sun exposure, heat stress and unnoticed changes in vital signs can affect health, especially in older adults, outdoor workers and people on medications that increase sun sensitivity. AURA's idea of bringing UV exposure and vital indicators together in one simple composite score is thoughtful and relevant to everyday preventive care.

I was particularly impressed by the personalised skin sensitivity mode and the focus on simple, human-friendly guidance. The concept shows a mature understanding of prevention for someone so young and it reflects the spirit of UN Goal of good health and well-being.

I am happy to recommend Project AURA for consideration in this competition and encourage them to continue developing the project.

As a Consultant dermatologist with 15-20 years of experience, I see every day how the skin reflects the story of a person's lifetime of habits and exposures. Many of the conditions I treat, including sunburn, pigmentation, premature ageing and other skin related issues, are linked to cumulative UV exposure that's often hard to track.

This is why I was so impressed by Armaan's project, AURA. At only 11 years old, he has chosen to focus on UV exposure, something most people only think about after the damage is done. He has also understood that skin is not separate from the rest of the body. Sun exposure, heat and overall health are connected and he has built a prototype that looks at them together.

I appreciated several aspects of his work:

1. Cumulative exposure tracking: UV damage accumulates over time, and an objective exposure record is more reliable than recall.

2. Skin sensitivity mode: Susceptibility to UV varies by skin type, so personalised guidance is clinically sensible.

3. Appropriate scope: The project is positioned as an awareness tool and not a substitute for medical advice.

As with any prototype, further testing and validation would be needed before it could be used more widely. Even so, the curiosity and maturity he has shown are admirable.

Congratulations, Armaan on representing India. Keep learning and caring about the health of others. I wish you every success.

I am Dr. Anupama Gupta, a practising paediatrician with over 35 years of experience and I am glad to review your project, AURA.

Children's skin is thinner and more sensitive than adult skin, and repeated high sun exposure in childhood may cause health repercussions later in life. Parents often cannot tell how much sun exposure their child is really getting and children rarely think about it at all. A wearable device that makes UV exposure visible, adjusts to skin sensitivity and alerts both the child and a parent is a caring and practical idea.

I particularly appreciate the skin sensitivity mode. Children differ widely in skin type, and a fair-skinned child needs earlier warnings than a darker-skinned one. Personalised alerts are far more useful than a one-size-fits-all number. AURA looks at the child's overall wellbeing in the sun, not just skin protection. I also like the composite safety score. It gives families one simple, easy-to-understand signal instead of several numbers to interpret.

Your work in this area is commendable. Congratulations on representing India at such a young age. Keep asking questions, testing your ideas and learning from doctors and other experts. Wishing you every success at the Fibonacci International Robot Olympiad.
